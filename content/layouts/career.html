[ Process Notes — Origin Stage ]
Brought on through AmeriCorps with a background shaped by the Alabama Gulf Coast, where clean waterways were simply part of life. That personal baseline proved to be an asset. Assigned to monitor the Jones Falls, Gwynns Falls, and Patapsco River watershed, the work centered on stormwater outfall screening for sewage contamination, ambient water quality monitoring, and community reporting — methodical, unglamorous, and genuinely necessary. The contrast between what healthy watersheds look like and what Baltimore had normalized gave the work a quiet urgency that showed up every day on the job.

[ Process Notes — Hurricane Maria Emergency Response ]
During that same period, in the fall of 2017, volunteered with CrowdRescue HQ following Hurricane Maria's landfall in Puerto Rico. What began as a volunteer role escalated rapidly into serving as GIS infrastructure lead for the emergency response effort. Geospatial data assembled from satellite imagery, social media, field reports, and remote sensing was delivered daily to FEMA headquarters and the Chief of the Army Corps of Engineers. Also participated in a White House workshop on information flows during natural disasters and contributed to Geospatial Consortium standards for emergency response data. It was a high-stakes validation of everything built during those four years at MES.

[ Process Notes — Operator Reentry Stage ]
Entered the operator training program the same way every prior challenge had been approached — show up, pay attention, and figure out what needs building. The subject matter was new: biological and chemical treatment, process control, SCADA operation, regulatory compliance. The learning method was not. Structured progression from OIT to OIT-I over two years. A B.A. in Environmental Sustainability qualified for a reduced experience requirement, putting the Maryland 5A license in hand a full year ahead of the standard timeline.

[ Process Notes — Active Operations ]
Joined the Little Patuxent Water Reclamation Plant as an Operator I with the Maryland 5A license just two months away — a deliberate foot in the door at a new facility while the remaining time requirement cleared. Promotion to Operator II followed automatically upon receipt of the license. From there, pursued the Operator III position as soon as the role became available and the hiring criteria aligned, securing a classification that sits directly below the Supervisor and Superintendent level — meaningful positioning for what comes next.

Daily responsibilities span process control, biological and chemical treatment, SCADA monitoring, and regulatory compliance. On some shifts, operators holding temporary licenses work the facility under direct supervision — a legal requirement that places the licensed operator's credential and judgment at the center of every decision made during those hours. The Patuxent River and the Chesapeake Bay are both downstream. That fact is never far from mind. Actively mentors incoming apprentices through Howard County's operator training program, continuing a cycle that began with others investing in an entry-level hire not so long ago.
