[ Process Notes — Origin Stage ]
Entry point confirmed. Raised on the Alabama Gulf Coast, where clean water was a birthright, not an environmental cause. Relocated to Baltimore and found a city that had simply accepted its waterways were dirty. Joined Blue Water Baltimore through AmeriCorps to monitor the Jones Falls, Gwynns Falls, and Patapsco River watershed — stormwater outfall screening for sewage contamination, ambient water quality monitoring, community reporting. Unglamorous work. Necessary service. The contrast between what the Gulf Coast had and what Baltimore had accepted drove the mission forward. This is where purpose became direction.

[ Process Notes — Hurricane Maria Emergency Response ]
Hurricane Maria made landfall in Puerto Rico. Joined CrowdRescue HQ as a volunteer; escalated quickly to GIS infrastructure lead without warning. The geospatial data produced — assembled from satellite imagery, social media, field reports, remote sensing — reached FEMA HQ coordination desks and the Chief of the Army Corps of Engineers daily. Attended White House workshop on Information Flows During Natural Disasters. Contributed to Geospatial Consortium standards for emergency response data. Self-taught capability validated at federal scale. The fear that information didn't reach the right channels in time — that it cost lives — has not faded. It never will.

[ Process Notes — Operator Reentry Stage ]
Entered the operator training program with the same approach that had worked before: show up, pay attention, figure out what needs building. The subject matter was new — biological and chemical treatment, process control, SCADA operation, regulatory compliance — but the learning method was familiar. People questioned why someone with this background was starting from the bottom. The technical material proved not to be the obstacle. Two years of structured progression, OIT to OIT-I. The plant environment suited the same systematic thinking GIS work had required: complex interdependent systems, real consequences for error. The B.A. in Environmental Sustainability qualified for a reduced experience requirement — two years instead of the standard three — putting the 5A license in hand a full year ahead of schedule. Exited fully licensed. The pivot had worked.

[ Process Notes — Active Operations ]
Operator I to III in under two years. Maryland Wastewater 5A — state maximum. Daily operations at the Little Patuxent Water Reclamation Plant: process control, biological and chemical treatment, SCADA monitoring, regulatory compliance. Some shifts running the facility with operators on temporary licenses working under this license — a responsibility taken seriously. The Patuxent River is downstream. The Chesapeake Bay is downstream. That fact is never far from mind. Active mentorship of incoming apprentices through Howard County's operator training program. A career that began with someone investing in entry has come full circle. The watershed is unchanged. The mission never was.
